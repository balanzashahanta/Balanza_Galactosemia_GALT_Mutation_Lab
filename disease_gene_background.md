# Disease and Normal Gene Function

## Disease Background

Classic galactosemia is an inherited disorder of galactose metabolism caused by reduced activity of the galactose-1-phosphate uridylyltransferase (GALT) enzyme. GALT deficiency disrupts the normal Leloir pathway of galactose metabolism and can lead to the accumulation of galactose-1-phosphate and other metabolites. Clinical manifestations can include jaundice, liver dysfunction, feeding difficulties, failure to thrive, and other complications if untreated.

## GALT Gene and Normal Protein

The **GALT** gene encodes galactose-1-phosphate uridylyltransferase, an enzyme involved in the Leloir pathway. The enzyme catalyzes the conversion of galactose-1-phosphate and UDP-glucose into UDP-galactose and glucose-1-phosphate. Normal GALT activity is therefore important for the proper metabolism of galactose.
